Noise Study

QuadSAT Z3U Noise Study

QuadSAT ApS, Odense, Denmark · September 2026 – present

  • C
  • Epiq Sidekiq SDK
  • Matchstiq Z3u
  • MATLAB
  • Y-factor method
  • aarch64 cross-compiling

Built the measurement chain for QuadSAT’s SDR noise study: a C tool that reads average receive power from an Epiq Matchstiq Z3u software-defined radio, and MATLAB analysis that turns hot and cold noise-source sweeps into a noise-figure estimate with the Y-factor method.

  • Terminated-port noise power reads about −102 to −103 dBm in an 8 MHz bandwidth, close to the roughly −105 dBm thermal floor; used as the sanity check on the whole chain, calibration sign included.
  • Frequency-sweep tool writes each run to CSV with a configuration header, so the analysis refuses to compare hot and cold sweeps taken with different gain, bandwidth or sample settings.
  • Noise figure follows the Y-factor method, with guards that stop the analysis instead of printing a number when the two sweeps do not match or the Y-factor is too small to trust, and a self-test on synthetic data.
  • Found and fixed a configuration bug that made the second receive channel return only zeros (the RFIC must be put in dual-channel mode), and a pointer bug inherited from the SDK example that discarded the first IQ pairs of every block.
  • Measured how long the reading takes to settle at each gain (under 1 ms at high gain, up to about 10 ms at low gain) and set the defaults for settling time and sample count from it.

Underground Wireless Sensor Network for Mining Operations

Senior Capstone for Alpha Metallurgical Resources, The University of Alabama · August 2025 – April 2026

  • Ansys HFSS
  • Sionna RT
  • IEEE 802.11
  • IPv4 static routing
  • Fiber backhaul

Contracted by Alpha Metallurgical Resources, through UA’s new Senior Capstone initiative, to design a wireless sensor network for real-time data transfer in underground mining environments. The network is platform agnostic, for future use in thermography and personnel tracking.

  • Developed a low-latency (<50 ms), high-throughput (10 Mbps surge), platform-agnostic wireless network with Alpha’s engineering team and university faculty.
  • Evaluated signal propagation in a mine environment with various antennas in Ansys HFSS, and ran Sionna RT ray tracing to assess multipath reflections and channel responses.
  • Designed a fiber-wireless network: point-to-point IEEE 802.11 at Layer 2, IPv4 static routing at Layer 3, and a fiber downlink from the control room to the head of the underground sensor daisy chain for reliable backhaul.

Impedance Spectroscopy

Fractional-Order Circuit Model of Sucrose Solution Impedance

With Roman Sotner, Jan Jerabek and Todd J. Freeborn · Published at IEEE SoutheastCon 2025

  • Impedance spectroscopy
  • Fractional-order modeling
  • Particle swarm optimization
  • MATLAB
  • Keysight E4990A

Investigated how adding sucrose, up to 785 mM, to a liquid (unsweetened FuzeTea) changes its electrical impedance, as a possible method for food quality monitoring. Impedance was measured from 100 Hz to 1 MHz with gold and platinum electrodes, and a fractional-order equivalent circuit was fit to the data by numerical optimization.

  • The fitted model reproduced the measurements to within 2% in impedance magnitude and 6% in phase.
  • The liquid-resistance parameter roughly doubled as sucrose rose from 0 to 785 mM (from about 750 Ω to about 1.5 kΩ), making it a candidate marker for sucrose adulterants.
  • Fit the gold and platinum datasets simultaneously with MATLAB particle swarm optimization, to separate the liquid’s behaviour from the electrode interface.
  • The results suggest the model can be simplified by dropping the interface loss terms, since the fractional-order element already captures them.
